Chip relief after the hike — Friday is cooldown

Cash is closed overnight in Zurich. Thursday delivered the best session in weeks after oil and long yields eased: the Nasdaq rose 1.7% and semiconductors led. Friday equity futures are soft again, so the job is digestion, not chasing Thursday’s winners into the open.

01

Oil and yields eased; chips led the rebound

What happened

After Wednesday’s first Fed hike in three years to a 3.75%–4.00% funds target, Thursday cash reversed the scare. Brent settled about 1% lower near $104.82 a barrel, and the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to roughly 4.93% from about 5.01% late Wednesday. The Dow closed at 51,778.04 (+0.61%), the S&P 500 at 7,637.76 (+1.14%), and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,418.30 (+1.69%). The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 3.14% to 11,599.49, with 29 of 30 constituents higher.

What it means

The hike itself was widely expected; what mattered Thursday was the relief channel — softer oil and cooler long yields took pressure off growth duration. Semis and memory reclaimed leadership after earlier AI-spend jitters: Arm about +8.6%, Intel about +7.7%, AMD about +6.4%, SanDisk about +6.2%, Micron about +5.5%, with Nvidia about +2.5% and Broadcom about +2.3%. Foundry and memory supply-chain names moved with the tape (TSMC about +3.0%; storage peers Seagate and Western Digital also green). Rate- sensitive banks that sold hard Wednesday got a quieter digestion day rather than a full mean-reversion sprint. Energy producers face a third session of softer crude into Friday while prices remain above $100, so the inflation-input story is cooler, not closed.

Chart and trend facts

Verified Thursday closes only: Dow +0.61%, S&P +1.14%, Nasdaq +1.69%, SOX +3.14%. Early Friday wraps show equity futures modestly red after that bounce, with oil still above $100 but extending losses about 1% in overnight trade. Fed speakers Michelle Bowman and Jeffrey Schmid are on the Friday calendar for more path commentary.

What to do

Treat Thursday as a relief session after the hike, not a green light to add size into a soft Friday futures open. Prefer watching cash hours confirm before scaling chip or growth exposure that already ran hard.

02

Intel led on CPU scarcity — watch the chain, not the chase

What happened

Intel closed Thursday at $108.80, up 7.67%, one of the session’s clearest semiconductor leaders. CEO Lip-Bu Tan said at this week’s AI Infrastructure Summit that Intel is meeting only about half of frontier customer CPU demand as AI agent deployments surge, and that major tech CEOs have called asking for more shipments. Separately, wraps cited exploratory talks between Intel and SK Hynix on U.S. memory manufacturing; SK Hynix confirmed discussions but said no agreement is finalized. Peer tape backed the theme: AMD finished at $545.09 (+6.36%), Arm about +8.6%, and memory names Micron (+5.5% to $977.50) and SanDisk (+6.2%) joined the move.

What it means

The AI stack story is broadening again from GPU pacing debates into CPU scarcity and memory capacity. If Tan’s supply comment holds, beneficiaries sit across the chain — CPU vendors and their equipment partners, HBM and DDR suppliers, and foundry capacity (TSMC’s Thursday gain fits that map). Exploratory Hynix–Intel manufacturing talks are a sentiment boost for domestic memory buildout, not a booked deal. Dispersion still matters: Nvidia’s more modest +2.5% shows leadership rotated toward capacity- constrained CPU and memory names rather than a single mega-cap chase.
