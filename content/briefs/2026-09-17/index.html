Hike delivered; path keeps the pressure on

What happened

Wednesday’s FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75–4.00%, unanimously. The statement said inflation remains elevated and that the move supports a timelier return to the 2% goal. Updated projections put the median year-end rate near 4.1%, with most officials still seeing another hike later this year. Into the close, the Dow finished at 51,461.90 (−1.21%), the S&P 500 at 7,551.81 (−0.45%), and the Nasdaq Composite at 25,978.42 (−0.01%). The 10-year stayed on the 5% handle near 5.00–5.02%. Oil eased: WTI settled near $102.40 and Brent near $105.60–$105.80. Thursday morning in Europe, US equity futures were attempting a rebound after the cash selloff — roughly a few tenths of a percent higher into the Asia handoff, with Nasdaq futures leading the bounce.

What it means

The quarter-point print was not the surprise. The hawkish framing and another hike in the dots were. Three sessions of cash pressure into a higher terminal path keep duration and crowded growth on a shorter leash. Micron (MU), Bloom Energy (BE), Seagate (STX), Dell (DELL), and Siemens Energy (ENR.DE) remain the weekly-trend names to watch — not fresh entries on a futures bounce. Credit-sensitive hardware and AI betas stay exposed if speakers keep the “higher for longer” tone alive.
