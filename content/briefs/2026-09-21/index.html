01

Weekend geopolitics, Monday futures green

What happened

Friday’s cash tape was muted after Thursday’s chip relief. The Dow closed at 51,682.64 (−0.18%), the S&P 500 at 7,650.50 (+0.17%), and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,522.55 (+0.40%). For the week the Dow fell 1.7%, the S&P roughly 0.1%, and only the Nasdaq finished green (+0.7%). The 10-year Treasury yield finished Friday near 5.00%. Over the weekend Yemen’s Houthis claimed missile and drone strikes on Riyadh, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng held talks in New York ahead of Thursday’s Trump–Xi meeting. Into Monday’s Asia session, S&P 500 futures were about 0.4% higher and Nasdaq futures about 0.6% higher; South Korea’s tech-heavy index gained roughly 1.1%–1.5% while Japan stayed shut for Silver Week.

What it means

The week’s map is diplomacy versus energy risk. Tariff, AI-governance, and critical- minerals headlines around Trump–Xi support growth and semiconductor sentiment if talks stay constructive — Asia’s AI-linked tape already lifted overnight. The Houthi strike keeps Middle East shipping and crude volatility in play even as Monday wraps showed oil easing on talk of recovering Saudi export flows (Brent near $101.63, about −2.1%; WTI near $98.15). Sticky yields near 5% and roughly 56% odds of another Fed hike in October still pressure rate-sensitive banks and small caps after the Dow’s third losing week. Breadth on Friday was weak — decliners led advancers — so a soft-green futures open does not erase last week’s digestion.

Chart and trend facts

What to do

Treat the overnight bounce as positioning into a diplomacy-heavy week, not confirmation that the hawkish path or energy risk is cleared. Prefer watching cash hours before adding size.

02

Storage led Friday’s AI follow-through

What happened

Inside Friday’s mixed indexes, storage and AI infrastructure names kept the chip-relief baton. Seagate closed at $858.79, up 6.93% from Thursday’s $803.13. Western Digital rose about 4.1% to $441.36, Broadcom about 3.0% to $357.61, and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF about 2.2% to $573.00. Nvidia added about 1.3% to $222.27. Dispersion stayed loud: Qualcomm fell about 5.8% to $177.72 and Dell about 3.5% to $568.06 after stronger prior sessions — the same AI stack, different outcomes inside one Friday.

What it means

AI demand is still showing up in capacity hardware, not only in GPU headlines. Storage vendors (Seagate, Western Digital) are direct supply-chain beneficiaries when data-center buildouts keep absorbing drives and nearline capacity; Broadcom sits on the networking and custom-silicon side of the same map. Asia’s Monday lift in tech-heavy indexes fits that channel if Trump–Xi talk stays constructive on AI and critical minerals. The Qualcomm and Dell pullbacks are a reminder that Thursday–Friday chip relief was not a blank check — names that already ran can digest while peers extend.
